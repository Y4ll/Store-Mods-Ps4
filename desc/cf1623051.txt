## Description

There's a particular kind of dread that comes from weather that never breaks a a sky the color of an old bruise, a fog bank that swallows the tree line thirty blocks out, water you wouldn't want to put your hand into. Horror Hill Visual Shader takes that feeling and builds it directly into the bones of the world. This is not a texture pack. Nothing about the shape of a block, the pattern on a plank, or the sprite on a mob changes. What changes is the air between you and everything else a the fog, the light, the color, the sky, the water a so that a world you already know by heart starts to feel like somewhere you shouldn't be standing after dark.

Built entirely on Minecraft Bedrock's Vibrant Visuals rendering system, this pack rewrites the game's fog, lighting, color grading, atmospheric sky settings, and water appearance across every biome in the game, from sun-choked plains to the dead calm of the End. It is a full atmosphere overhaul rather than a partial reskin, and because it never touches a single block or item texture, it is built to run cleanly underneath almost any texture pack you already have installed.

### The Fog System

At the heart of the pack are nine separate fog definitions, each tuned for a different kind of place. The default overworld fog rolls in thick and close, cutting real visibility down to a fraction of the vanilla draw distance and tinting everything a sickly, washed-out grey-green. Ocean and wetland biomes a oceans, rivers, swamps, mushroom shores a get a denser, murkier variant, because there is nothing worse than fog you cannot see the bottom of. Frozen and alpine biomes get a paler, colder fog that still oppresses without losing its icy character.

The Nether does not get a single fog treatment; it gets five. Basalt Deltas sit under a choking, ash-grey haze. The Nether wastes and Soul Sand Valley are wrapped in a dead, faded fog that drains the usual fire-orange energy out of the place, leaving something closer to a bone yard. Crimson Forest carries a deep, arterial red murk, and Warped Forest a sickly, sunken teal, so each Nether biome keeps its own visual identity while still feeling wrong in its own particular way. The End, meanwhile, is wrapped in a near-black violet fog that swallows the horizon almost entirely, turning an already alien biome into something closer to a void with teeth.

Every fog file in the pack also carries its own volumetric density settings, not just simple distance fog, so the haze has genuine depth and thickness hanging in the air rather than sitting as a flat color wash at the edge of the render distance.

### Lighting That Feels Dead

Vanilla Minecraft's daylight is bright and cheerful almost by design. Horror Hill strips that out completely. The sun's brightness curve has been rewritten so that even at in-game noon, the light feels overcast and thin a never the harsh, golden light players are used to a and its color has been pushed toward pale, bruised greys instead of warm white. Ambient light, the light that fills shadows and corners even when nothing is casting a direct beam, has been dropped significantly and tinted a dull, olive-grey, so indoor spaces and cave mouths sit in genuine gloom rather than vanilla's usual forgiving fill light.
