A short crusaders summary:

The Crusades were a series of wars authorized by the Latin Church between 1095/99 and the 13th century. They were driven by a mix of religious, strategic, and economic motives. In the narrow sense, the term refers to campaigns against Muslim states in the Near East, especially to regain Jerusalem and secure Christian access to holy sites. After the First Crusade captured Jerusalem in 1099, several Crusader states were founded in the Levant, but most later crusades achieved limited success. Jerusalem was lost again in 1187 after the Battle of Hattin, and the last major Crusader stronghold, Acre, fell in 1291.

Over time, the term Crusade was expanded to include other military campaigns, including wars against non-Christian peoples, heretics, and even political enemies within Christianity. Although religion was a central motivation, the Crusades were also used for political power and territorial ambitions. The participants often saw themselves as carrying out a holy pilgrimage and a just war in service of God, even though the campaigns were frequently marked by extreme violence.
