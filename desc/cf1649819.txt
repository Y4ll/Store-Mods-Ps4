ANCIENT VILLAGERS

38 items: currencies, relics, crowns, weapons, working shields, food, the war horn, the imperial decree, ancient scrolls
24 custom mobs and professions: warriors, cavalry and emperors (seven types)
Invasions with an objective tracker
Wild skirmishes between rival war bands
Festivals, coronations and mourning bells

Seven Civilisations -

Greek
Marble, hoplites, the Owl of Athena

Roman
Legionaries, the scutum and gladius, Saturnalia

Egyptian
Sandstone temples, the pschent, khopesh-armed Medjay

Persian
The King of Kings, Immortals, Nowruz

Norse
Longhouses, bearded axes, the Winter Blot

Maya
Stepped pyramids, the macuahuitl, the Wayeb rites

Carthaginian
Harbour cities, the Sign of Tanit, Punic shields

Each has its own architecture, temple, palace, statue, currency, food, festival, weapons, shields, iron golem, and trade tables for all 13 professions.

The add-on rebuilds your world's villages.

Conquest -

Wear a people's crown and fight for them. Defend their cities, kill the raiders at their gates, bring gifts to their court.

Their army will march on your command. Pick a rival city and conquer!

Cities pay tax, and revolt if you neglect them.

The Imperial Codex -

Your holdings, your standing with all seven peoples, your campaigns, and every city you have found.

Sneak and interact with a ruler for his court. A click still trades.

Ancient Professions -

New villager professions with their own robes and trades in every civilisation.

- Philosopher at the temple. Books, scrolls, bottled experience.

- Astrologer at the statue. Clocks, compasses, spyglasses. Ask him and he tells you when an army is coming.

- Chronicler at the palace. Maps, name tags, and the city's history: who took it, and what it was called before.

Getting started -

- Find a village. It rises on its own.

- Defend it when it is attacked.

- Get a crown and wear it.

- Sneak and use the crown to open the Codex (more features when interacting with emperor)

/scriptevent anc:codex opens it anywhere. /scriptevent anc:diag reports what the add-on can see.
