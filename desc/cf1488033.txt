A complete survival overhaul that adds two interconnected systems to your world: thirst and temperature. Stay hydrated, dress for the weather, and watch your environment, every biome, every block, and every decision matters.

Features

Guide Book

Every player gets a Water & Temperature Guide on their first join, with a chapter on each system: thirst and what drains it, finding and purifying water, the five temperature states, which armor actually keeps you warm, and how weather works.

Lost it? Craft it back from a book and paper at a crafting table.

The difficulty selector lives at the top of the guide.

Difficulty

Thirst and temperature have their own difficulty, chosen in the guide book and shared by everyone on the world.

- Easy Thirst drains slower, hot and cold extremes are pulled in, and freezing and heat damage are halved. Ordinary rain will chill you but won't freeze you.

- Normal The pack as designed.

- Hard Thirst drains faster, the extremes are pushed further out, and temperature damage is increased.

- Match World Difficulty The default. Follows your world's difficulty setting, exactly as previous versions did.

Temperature System

Your body temperature changes dynamically based on the world around you. A thermometer HUD displays beside your water bar, shifting through five states: Very Cold, Cold, Normal, Hot, and Very Hot.

What affects your temperature:

- Biome detection The system scans 100+ block types around you to determine the biome temperature. Deserts and badlands scorch you, snowy tundras freeze you, and forests keep things mild.

- Day/night cycle Midday is warmest, night is coldest. Plan your expeditions accordingly.

- Altitude High mountains are freezing. Deep underground is warmer.

- Shelter A roof over your head insulates you, pulling temperature back toward normal.

- Heat sources Campfires, lava, torches, lanterns, furnaces, and fire all radiate warmth with realistic distance falloff.

- Weather Rain, storms and snowfall chill you under open sky, and the longer you stay out the worse it gets.

- Water exposure Swimming makes you colder over time. Deeper water is worse. Stay in too long and hypothermia sets in progressively.

- Armor Leather is the best cold-weather gear. But heavy metal armor in a desert? That traps heat and makes you even hotter. Netherite is the exception it insulates poorly, but it won't cook you.

- Activity Moving warms you a little. Sprinting warms you more.

- Dimension-aware The Nether is always scorching. The End is always cold.

Temperature States:

- Very Cold Slowness II, mining fatigue, freezing damage over time. A frost overlay covers your screen.

- Cold Slowness I. A warning to find warmth.

- Normal No effects. You're comfortable.

- Hot Hunger effect. You're sweating.

- Very Hot Hunger II, weakness, fire damage over time. A heat overlay fills your screen edges.

Transitions between states are smooth and gradual, no jarring instant swaps.

Fire Resistance blocks heat damage and its debuffs outright useful for Nether trips.

Weather & Getting Wet

Weather is a real threat, not a backdrop.

- Rain builds Standing under open sky in rain gets colder the longer you stay in it, rather than hitting a cap straight away. A full soak takes around a minute.

- Storms are worse Thunderstorms chill harder than plain rain, night is colder than day, and rain falling as snow in a cold biome is colder than either.

- You stay wet Getting under cover doesn't dry you instantly. The chill lingers at half strength while you dry off.

- Fire dries you faster Standing near a campfire or furnace dries you several times quicker than waiting it out.

- Shelter works Any solid roof keeps the rain off you entirely. Leaves and glass don't count.
